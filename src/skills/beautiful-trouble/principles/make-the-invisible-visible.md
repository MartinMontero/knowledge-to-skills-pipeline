# Make the Invisible Visible

> "What the eye doesn't see, the heart doesn't grieve over — so make it impossible not to see."

## Overview

Many injustices persist precisely because they are abstract, distant, or hidden: a statistic, a line in a budget, a harm that happens out of sight. This principle is about giving the invisible a concrete, sensory form — a physical representation, an image, a number made tangible — so that the public can finally perceive what was always there.

## When to Use

**Best for:**
- Issues described mainly in numbers (debt, emissions, casualties, wait times)
- Harms that happen far away or behind closed doors
- Slow problems that never produce a single dramatic "news moment"
- Building empathy where statistics produce numbness

## Techniques

### 1. Make the number physical
Translate an abstract quantity into an object people can stand next to: one shoe for each life lost, a stack representing a debt, a line of chairs for a waiting list.

### 2. Relocate the harm into view
Bring the hidden thing to where decision-makers and the public are: stage the distant consequence on the doorstep of the institution responsible.

### 3. Personify the abstraction
Give a face and a story to a statistic. One concrete person, honestly represented, can carry what a million data points cannot.

### 4. Use time-lapse or accumulation
Show change that's normally too slow to see — before/after, a growing tally, a clock counting the cost in real time.

## Worked Example

**Issue:** A community's water has unsafe lead levels that residents can't see.

- Fill transparent jugs with the actual tap water and line them outside city hall, each labeled with an address and its lead reading.
- A clean control jug sits at the end for contrast.
- The invisible — a number on a lab report — becomes a wall of cloudy water with neighbors' names on it.

## Strategic Notes

- **Pair with a demand.** Visibility creates attention; attention without an ask dissipates. Always connect the image to the specific change you want (see Power Analysis Framework).
- **Stay truthful.** The power of this principle is credibility. Exaggeration that's later debunked destroys the message.
- **Design for the photograph.** Ask: what's the single image that tells the whole story? Build the action around it.

## Safety & Ethics

| Concern | Mitigation |
|---------|------------|
| Representing real victims | Get consent; never exploit people's suffering for spectacle |
| Misleading visuals | Keep representations accurate and sourced |
| Public-space rules | Check permits and local law; keep it lawful and non-violent |

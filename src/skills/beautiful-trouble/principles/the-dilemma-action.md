# The Dilemma Action

> "A good dilemma action means you win whether they say yes or no."

## Overview

A dilemma action is structured so that every option available to your target advances your goal. If they permit the action, you accomplish it openly. If they suppress it, the suppression itself dramatizes the injustice and wins sympathy. The target is caught between two losing moves — that's the dilemma.

## When to Use

**Best for:**
- Targets who rely on a reasonable public image and can't afford to look repressive
- Situations where the underlying injustice is hidden and needs to be made visible
- Moments when you have a small, disciplined group ready to act with clear messaging

## Designing the Dilemma

### 1. Identify the contradiction
Find the gap between what the target *says* and what they *do*. The dilemma forces them to honor their stated values or expose the contradiction.

### 2. Create a "decision point"
Build an action that requires the target to actively choose: allow it (you win) or stop it (they look bad). A permit request, a public offering, a symbolic act of care they'd have to forbid.

### 3. Make both outcomes a win
Write down, in advance, the script for each branch:
- **If they allow it:** what you accomplish and how you publicize it.
- **If they refuse/repress:** how the refusal becomes the story.

### 4. Keep the moral high ground
Dilemmas work because your side looks reasonable and the target looks unreasonable. Stay non-violent, lawful where possible, and clearly constructive.

## Classic Pattern

> Activists offer to *clean up* a contested public space, or *plant a garden*, or *return* something. The target must either let a wholesome act proceed (legitimizing the activists) or send authorities to stop people from doing something good (legitimizing the grievance).

## Worked Example

**Issue:** A city neglects a derelict lot while denying community use.

- **Action:** Residents announce a public day to plant a community garden on the lot.
- **If the city allows it:** the community gains a garden and proves the space can serve people.
- **If the city stops it:** images of officials uprooting a community garden tell the whole story.

Either way, the contradiction — "the city would rather the lot rot than let neighbors tend it" — is exposed.

## Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Mitigation |
|---------|------------|
| The target finds a third option | War-game responses in advance using a power analysis |
| The dilemma is too clever to read | Keep the message and the ask plain enough to fit a headline |
| Escalation risks participant safety | Plan de-escalation and legal support before acting |

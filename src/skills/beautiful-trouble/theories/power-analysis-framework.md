# Power Analysis Framework

> "Power is not a thing; it is a relationship." — organizing tradition

## Overview

Before you can change a situation, you need a clear-eyed map of who actually controls it. Power analysis is the practice of identifying decision-makers, the sources their power rests on, the people and institutions they depend on, and the specific points where pressure can move them. It turns "we're angry about X" into "here is who can grant our demand and here is how we reach them."

## When to Use

**Best for:**
- The opening phase of any campaign, before choosing tactics
- Deciding *who* to target (the person who can actually say yes), not just *what* you oppose
- Finding leverage when a target seems immovable
- Avoiding wasted energy on symbolic targets with no decision power

## The Method

### 1. Define the change you want
State the demand as a concrete action a specific person or body could take. "End the contract" is actionable; "corporate greed" is not.

### 2. Identify the decision-maker(s)
Who has the formal authority to grant the demand? Name real people and roles, not abstractions.

### 3. Map their sources of power
Power typically rests on a few pillars — money, legitimacy, expertise, relationships, control of resources, or the cooperation of others. List them.

### 4. Find their dependencies
Every power-holder depends on someone: customers, voters, donors, employees, reputation, regulators, suppliers. Dependencies are where leverage lives.

### 5. Locate the pillars of support
What institutions and groups keep the target in place? Which of them might be moved, divided, or withdrawn?

### 6. Choose your leverage points
Match your group's capacities (numbers, skills, relationships, moral authority) to the target's vulnerabilities. The intersection is your strategy.

## Worked Example

**Goal:** A landlord stops an unjust eviction.

| Step | Finding |
|------|---------|
| Decision-maker | The property-management company's regional director |
| Sources of power | Rental income, reputation with future tenants, banking relationships |
| Dependencies | Tenants paying rent, positive online reviews, city housing permits |
| Pillars of support | The bank financing the building, the city housing office |
| Leverage | Public reviews + a delegation to the housing office + media on the eviction story |

## Spectrum of Allies

For each actor, place them on a spectrum: active allies → passive allies → neutral → passive opposition → active opposition. **You rarely win by converting active opponents.** You win by shifting each group one step toward you — neutrals become passive allies, passive allies become active.

## Safety & Ethics

| Concern | Mitigation |
|---------|------------|
| Targeting individuals can become personal harassment | Focus on roles and decisions, not private lives; keep it lawful and non-violent |
| Bad maps lead to wasted campaigns | Test assumptions with people who know the institution from inside |
| Power maps go stale | Revisit after every major development |
